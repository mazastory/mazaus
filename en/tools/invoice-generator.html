International Commercial Invoice Requirements and Customs Clearance

A Commercial Invoice serves as the primary legal document utilized by foreign customs authorities (such as US Customs and Border Protection) to classify commodities, determine import duties, and verify trade compliance.

1. Essential Elements of Customs Clearance Compliance

  • Detailed Commodity Description: Vague terms such as "Sample" or "Parts" trigger mandatory physical inspection holds. Specify material composition, intended usage, and exact model names.
  • Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS / HS Code): Provide standard 6-digit Harmonized System codes to ensure correct tariff assessment.
  • Incoterms 2020: Clarify whether freight and cargo insurance are included in the declared customs value (e.g. CIF vs FOB).
  • Country of Origin: Clearly designate where the goods were manufactured or underwent substantial transformation.

2. Proforma Invoice vs Commercial Invoice

A Proforma Invoice is a preliminary quotation provided prior to formal production or shipment, allowing buyers to arrange letters of credit (L/C) or import permits. A Commercial Invoice is the final binding bill issued upon shipment departure.
